    LumaNav

    See the terrain. Predict the windows. Plan the landing.

    Rates lunar landing sites and dates on two things: power (the Sun is visible above the terrain) and communication (Earth is visible above the terrain).

    Data

    • Routes to the Moon (estimates based on real missions, not AI): Fast ~3 days (Apollo 11, 1969: ~76 h to lunar orbit), Standard ~5 days (Artemis I, 2022: launched 16 Nov, lunar flyby 21 Nov), Low-energy ~110 days (CAPSTONE, 2022: ~4.5 months). Δv per phase uses typical values. Moon position, transfer ellipse (Kepler) and propellant (Tsiolkovsky) are computed; the low-energy path in 3D is illustrative only.
    • Terrain: NASA LRO / LOLA (PDS): LDEM_75S_240M & LDEM_75N_240M (240 m per pixel, latitude ≥75.2°) and LDEM_16 (~1.9 km per pixel, whole Moon).
    • Sun & Earth positions: VSOP87 + IAU lunar rotation model (including libration) via astronomy-engine.
    • Earth textures: NASA Blue Marble Next Generation (via NASA GIBS) and NASA Black Marble 2016 (city lights).
    • Moon texture (3D mission & site picker): NASA SVS CGI Moon Kit, LRO LROC colour mosaic.

    Method

    1. From the landing point, rays are cast in 720 directions (every 0.5°) out to 150 km over the active LOLA map (240 m polar projection at the poles, global geodesics elsewhere). The steepest terrain angle in each direction, including lunar curvature, forms the horizon line.
    2. Every 30 minutes for 30 days, the Sun and Earth positions in the local sky are computed (azimuth & elevation).
    3. Sun (0.53° disc) above the horizon = sunlit. Earth above the horizon = direct communication possible.
    4. Verdicts and recommendations use a transparent, deterministic formula (no AI). Sites are ranked over the first 7 surface days after landing on a 0–100 scale: sunlit at touchdown (+20), Earth visible at touchdown (+10), share of time with both (+35), share of time sunlit (+20), and continuous light at the start of the mission (+15). A site whose elevation map (DEM) is not loaded can never top the ranking.

    Limitations

    • This is an analysis of lighting and line-of-sight geometry, not a guarantee of landing success or radio link quality.
    • Between 75.2°S and 75.2°N the global 16 pixel/degree map (~1.9 km/pixel) is used, so hills or craters smaller than ~2 km are not resolved as finely as at the poles (240 m/pixel).
    • The terrain colour layers use a coarser calculation (2°, 60 km) for speed, so their values are approximate.
    • In the orbit view, relief is exaggerated 2× (±40 km patch at the poles, ±120 km on the global map) and Earth and Sun are drawn 3× larger for clarity. The surface view uses true size (scroll to zoom), and all numbers use true scale.
    • Earth: phase from the real Sun direction, rotation from Greenwich sidereal time (sub-Moon point within ~0.4° of JPL Horizons). Clouds are not drawn.
    • Coordinates of some commercial landers are approximate.
    • The mission planner uses typical Δv per phase and the Tsiolkovsky rocket equation: an early-planning estimate, not trajectory optimisation. The trajectory diagram is schematic.
